Common CRM features

Modern CRMs typically include:

  • Contact and account management
  • Lead capture and qualification
  • Sales pipeline management
  • Opportunity tracking
  • Task and activity management
  • Email integration (Gmail, Outlook)
  • Calendar synchronization
  • Quotes and invoices
  • Marketing automation
  • Customer support/ticketing
  • Workflow automation
  • Reporting and dashboards
  • AI-assisted insights and forecasting
  • Mobile apps
  • Role-based access control
  • APIs and third-party integrations

Typical technology stack behind a modern CRM

Large CRM systems are commonly built with technologies such as:

  • Frontend: React, Angular, Vue.js
  • Backend: Node.js, Java (Spring Boot), C# (.NET), Go
  • Databases: PostgreSQL, MySQL, SQL Server, MongoDB
  • Search: Elasticsearch or OpenSearch
  • Caching: Redis
  • Messaging: Kafka, RabbitMQ, Amazon SQS
  • Authentication: OAuth 2.0, OpenID Connect, SAML
  • Cloud: AWS, Azure, Google Cloud
  • AI: Large language models for email drafting, lead scoring, chatbots, meeting summaries, and forecasting

If you're interviewing as a Senior Full Stack Developer

The CRM concepts interviewers most often expect you to understand are:

  • Lead → Opportunity → Customer lifecycle
  • Accounts vs. Contacts
  • Sales pipelines and stages
  • Activity tracking (calls, emails, meetings)
  • Role-based permissions
  • Workflow automation
  • REST APIs and webhooks
  • Reporting and analytics
  • Third-party integrations (Google, Microsoft 365, Slack, payment providers)
  • Multi-tenant architecture
  • Audit logs and data security
  • Scalable database design
  • Event-driven systems and background job processing

For senior backend or full-stack roles, it's common to be asked to design a CRM module (such as lead management or a sales pipeline), model the database schema, design REST APIs, and discuss how you would scale the system for millions of customers.

Recommendation for a full-stack developer

Since you have a full-stack background, EspoCRM or SuiteCRM are excellent choices if you want to learn how enterprise CRMs are built. They expose REST APIs, support custom modules, workflows, role-based permissions, and database customization. If your goal is simply to use a CRM with minimal setup, HubSpot's free plan is the easiest place to start while still reflecting workflows commonly used in industry.
