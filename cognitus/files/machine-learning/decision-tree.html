Decision Tree

A Decision Tree is a machine learning model that makes decisions by asking a series of yes/no questions, just like a flowchart.

Think of it as playing the game "20 Questions."

Simple Example

Suppose you want to predict whether someone will buy a laptop.

The decision tree might ask:


                Income > $50,000?
                /           \
             Yes             No
            /                 \
     Age < 30?          Don't Buy
       /    \
     Yes    No
     /        \
   Buy      Don't Buy

How it works

  1. It starts at the root (first question).
  2. It asks a question about the data.
  3. Depending on the answer (Yes/No), it follows a branch.
  4. It keeps asking questions until it reaches a leaf (final prediction).

Real-world Example

Imagine a bank deciding whether to approve a loan.


Has a job?
    |
   Yes
    |
Credit score > 700?
    |
  Yes -----> Approve Loan
    |
   No
    |
Income > $40,000?
    |
 Yes -----> Approve Loan
 No ------> Reject Loan

Why is it called a "Tree"?

It looks like an upside-down tree:

  • Root = first decision
  • Branches = possible answers
  • Leaves = final prediction

Advantages

  • ✅ Easy to understand
  • ✅ Easy to visualize
  • ✅ Works with numbers and categories
  • ✅ Doesn't require much data preparation

Disadvantages

  • ❌ Can become very large and complicated
  • ❌ Can memorize the training data (overfitting)
  • ❌ Small changes in data can produce a different tree

Simple Analogy

Imagine deciding what to wear:


Is it raining?
    |
  Yes
    |
Take an umbrella
    |
Is it cold?
   |
 Yes --> Wear a jacket
 No --> Wear a T-shirt

You're making decisions step by step—this is exactly how a decision tree works.

In Machine Learning

  • Input (Features): Age, income, education, credit score
  • Questions: "Is age > 30?", "Is income > $50,000?"
  • Output (Prediction): Buy/Don't Buy, Spam/Not Spam, Approve/Reject

A decision tree learns these questions automatically from training data to make predictions on new, unseen data.

Yes. A Decision Tree is a predictive machine learning model.

Its main purpose is to predict an output for new data based on patterns it learned from training data.

Example 1: Classification (Predicting a Category)

Suppose you train a decision tree with past customer data.

Age Income Bought Laptop
25 High Yes
40 Low No
30 High Yes

After training, you give it a new customer:

  • Age = 28
  • Income = High

The model predicts:

➡️ Bought Laptop = Yes

Here, it's predicting a category ("Yes" or "No").

Example 2: Regression (Predicting a Number)

Decision trees can also predict numerical values.

Training data:

House Size (sq ft) Price
1000 $150,000
1500 $220,000
2000 $300,000

For a new house:

  • Size = 1800 sq ft

The decision tree might predict:

➡️ Price = $270,000

Here, it's predicting a number.

Why is it called predictive?

The model learns from historical (training) data and then predicts outcomes for new, unseen data.


Past Data
    │
    ▼
Decision Tree learns patterns
    │
    ▼
New Data
    │
    ▼
Prediction

Is it always accurate?

No. Like all machine learning models, a decision tree makes its best prediction based on the patterns it learned. If the training data is incomplete, noisy, or not representative, its predictions may be wrong.

In short:

  • ✅ Yes, a decision tree is a predictive machine learning model.
  • ✅ It can predict categories (classification), such as spam vs. not spam.
  • ✅ It can also predict numbers (regression), such as house prices or sales forecasts.
