Neuron

A neuron is a specialized cell in the nervous system that receives, processes, and transmits information using electrical and chemical signals.

Main Parts of a Neuron

Neurons are specialized cells in the nervous system that transmit information using electrical and chemical signals. They are the basic functional units of the brain, spinal cord, and nerves.

  1. Dendrites – receive signals from other neurons.
  2. Cell body (soma) – contains the nucleus and processes information.
  3. Axon – carries electrical impulses away from the cell body.
  4. Myelin sheath – insulating layer that speeds signal transmission.
  5. Axon terminals / synapses – pass signals to other neurons or muscles using neurotransmitters.

How Neurons Communicate

  • A neuron receives input through dendrites.
  • If the signal is strong enough, it generates an electrical impulse called an action potential.
  • The impulse travels along the axon.
  • At the synapse, chemicals called neurotransmitters carry the message to the next cell.

Types of Neurons

  • Sensory neurons – carry information from sensory organs to the brain/spinal cord.
  • Motor neurons – send commands from the brain/spinal cord to muscles.
  • Interneurons – connect neurons within the brain and spinal cord.

Interesting Facts

  • The human brain contains roughly 86 billion neurons.
  • Neurons can form thousands of connections called synapses.
  • Learning and memory involve strengthening or changing these connections, a property known as neuroplasticity.
