Crypto is short for cryptocurrency

Crypto is short for cryptocurrency — a type of digital money that uses cryptography and a computer network to control ownership and transactions.

A simple way to think about it:

Crypto = digital value + a shared network + rules for transferring that value

Example: Bitcoin

Bitcoin is the most famous cryptocurrency.

Suppose you have 1 Bitcoin.

Instead of a bank having a database saying:

"Paul owns 1 Bitcoin."

a network of computers collectively maintains a record of transactions showing that the Bitcoin belongs to your digital wallet.

When you send some Bitcoin to someone:

  1. You create a transaction.
  2. You digitally sign it using your private key.
  3. The transaction is broadcast to the network.
  4. Network participants verify that it's valid.
  5. The transaction becomes part of the blockchain.
  6. The recipient can now prove ownership of the transferred Bitcoin.

Why does crypto have value?

This connects directly to your earlier question about why anything has value.

Bitcoin doesn't have value because the coins themselves are physically useful.

Its value comes from people collectively believing:

"I can own this, transfer it, and other people will accept it as valuable."

That's actually similar to ordinary money.

For example, a ₱1,000 bill is just paper. Its value comes from a social and economic system that says:

"This represents ₱1,000 of purchasing power."

Crypto attempts to create a similar system without requiring a central bank or a single company to maintain the ownership ledger.

The important distinction

Crypto isn't necessarily the same thing as blockchain.

Blockchain = the technology for maintaining a shared transaction history.

Cryptocurrency = a digital asset/currency that can operate using that technology.

Think of it like:

Blockchain → accounting system

Bitcoin → asset recorded in that accounting system

And not all cryptocurrencies work exactly like Bitcoin. Some are designed for payments, some for applications, some for stable value, and some mainly for speculation.
