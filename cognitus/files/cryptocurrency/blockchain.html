Blockchain

Blockchain is a shared digital record book that allows many computers to agree on and keep a history of transactions without needing one central authority to control the record.

A simple way to imagine it:

Blockchain = a shared spreadsheet/database that thousands of computers maintain together, where past records are extremely difficult to secretly change.

1. Traditional database

Normally, a company might have one central database:


Bank
  ↓
Central Database
  ↓
Your balance: $1,000

The bank controls the database. It can add, modify, or remove records according to its rules.

2. Blockchain

With a blockchain, copies of the record are distributed across many computers:


                ┌── Computer A
                │
                ├── Computer B
   Blockchain ──┼── Computer C
                │
                ├── Computer D
                │
                └── Computer E

The computers communicate with each other and agree on which transactions are valid.

Why is it called a "blockchain"?

Transactions are grouped into blocks.

For example:


Block 1
 ├─ Alice → Bob: $10
 ├─ John → Mary: $25
 └─ Tom → Sarah: $5
        ↓
Block 2
 ├─ Bob → Alice: $3
 ├─ Mary → David: $15
 └─ ...
        ↓
Block 3
 └─ ...

Each block is cryptographically connected to the previous block:


Block 1 → Block 2 → Block 3 → Block 4 → ...

That's the chain in blockchain.

If someone tries to secretly change an old transaction, the cryptographic relationships no longer match, making the alteration detectable.

The important concept: consensus

This connects directly to your previous question about cryptocurrency.

Suppose someone says:

"I own 100 Bitcoin."

How does the network know whether that's true?

The computers participating in the blockchain follow a consensus mechanism—rules for determining which transactions are legitimate and what the current state of the ledger is.

For example:


    Person A: "Alice sends Bob 1 coin."
             ↓
       Network checks:
       - Does Alice have it?
       - Is the transaction valid?
       - Has Alice already spent it?
             ↓
       Network agrees
             ↓
       Transaction recorded

So your earlier idea was essentially correct:

A cryptocurrency works because a network of participants agrees on the rules and collectively maintains the ledger.

Blockchain vs. cryptocurrency

They're not the same thing.

Blockchain is the underlying technology.

Cryptocurrency is one application of that technology.

Think:


    Blockchain
    │
    ├── Bitcoin
    ├── Ethereum
    ├── Stablecoins
    └── Other applications

Bitcoin uses a blockchain to maintain its transaction history.

Why blockchain is useful

The interesting property is that you can have:

Digital ownership + shared verification + no single database administrator.

That can be useful for things like:

  • Cryptocurrency
  • Digital assets
  • Smart contracts
  • Tokenized assets
  • Supply-chain records
  • Certain financial settlement systems
  • Decentralized applications

But blockchain is not automatically better than a normal database. If one trusted organization can efficiently maintain the records, a traditional database is often faster, cheaper, and simpler.

The core idea

If you remember only one thing:

Blockchain is a decentralized, cryptographically linked ledger whose participants use consensus to agree on the state of the record.

And Bitcoin is essentially a monetary system built on top of this idea.
